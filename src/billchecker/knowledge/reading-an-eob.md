## What an EOB is
An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) is a statement from your health insurer after it processes a claim. It is not a bill. It shows what the provider charged, what your plan agreed to pay, and what you may owe. Insurers usually mail EOBs or post them in your online account. Your bill from the provider should match what the EOB says you owe.

## The columns on an EOB
Most EOBs have a row for each service with these amounts. Billed (or charged) is what the provider asked for. Allowed amount is the most your plan pays for that service under its contract with in-network providers. Plan paid is what the insurer paid the provider. You may owe (or patient responsibility) is your share: deductible, copay, and coinsurance. Many EOBs also show a remark or reason code that explains a denial or adjustment.

## Comparing the EOB with your bill
Match each service on the bill to a line on the EOB by date and code. Check that the amount the bill asks you to pay is not more than the EOB's "you may owe" total. If the bill asks for more, the provider may not have applied the insurance payment or adjustment yet, the bill may include other visits, or there may be a billing error. Call the billing office and have both documents in front of you.

## When the EOB arrives after the bill
Providers sometimes send a bill before the insurer finishes processing the claim. If you receive a bill but no EOB yet, you can ask the provider whether the claim was sent to your insurance and wait for the EOB before paying.
