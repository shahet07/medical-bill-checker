## Why claims get denied
Insurers deny claims for reasons such as missing information, a service the plan says is not covered or not medically necessary, a missing prior authorization, coding errors, or an out-of-network provider. The reason or remark code is usually printed on the EOB next to the denied service.

## Fixing simple denials first
Many denials come from billing or coding mistakes. Call the provider's billing office and ask whether the claim can be corrected and resubmitted. Also call your insurer and ask exactly why it was denied and what would change the decision.

## Internal appeal
For most plans covered by the Affordable Care Act, you can ask your insurer to reconsider in an internal appeal. You generally must file within 180 days of getting the denial notice. The insurer must decide within 30 days for a service you have not received yet and within 60 days for a service you already received. Urgent cases can be decided much faster, within 72 hours. Include a letter, the EOB, and any supporting records or a letter from your doctor.

## External review
If the internal appeal is denied, you can usually ask for an external review by an independent reviewer. You generally must request it within 4 months of the final internal denial. Rules differ for Medicare, Medicaid, some employer plans, and older grandfathered plans, so check your plan documents or ask your insurer which rules apply.
