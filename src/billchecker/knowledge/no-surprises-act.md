## What the law covers
The federal No Surprises Act, in effect since January 1, 2022, protects people with most kinds of health coverage from many surprise bills. It covers most emergency services, even at an out-of-network hospital; non-emergency services from out-of-network providers at an in-network hospital or ambulatory surgical center, such as anesthesia, radiology, or lab work; and air ambulance services. It does not cover ground ambulance rides.

## What you should pay in these cases
For protected services you should only pay your in-network cost sharing, meaning the copay, coinsurance, and deductible you would pay an in-network provider. The provider cannot balance bill you for the rest. For some non-emergency care, a provider can ask you to give up these protections by signing a consent form in advance, but they cannot ask for emergency care or for certain services like anesthesia.

## If you think a bill breaks these rules
Contact the provider and your insurer first and mention the No Surprises Act. You can also contact the No Surprises Help Desk run by CMS, listed at cms.gov/nosurprises, to ask questions or file a complaint. Many states have their own surprise billing laws as well.
